Vitamins Lab: Water-Soluble vs. Fat-Soluble Chemistry

Vitamins are organic molecules (they contain carbon), but they provide 0 kcal/g of energy. Instead, they act as coenzymes—tiny helper keys that unlock chemical reactions inside your cells.

WORKBOOK QUESTION 1 GUIDE Water-Soluble vs. Fat-Soluble

Why do fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) carry a higher risk of toxicity than water-soluble vitamins (B & C)?

a) Water-Soluble Fate (B-Complex & C): Dissolve directly in watery blood plasma. Your body only keeps what cells need right now; any extra mega-dose is filtered out by your kidneys (renal excretion) and leaves in your urine within hours.
b) Fat-Soluble Storage (Vitamins A, D, E, K): Stored inside your liver and adipose (fat) tissue. Because they cannot dissolve in watery urine, taking huge daily doses causes them to build up over weeks to toxic levels (called hypervitaminosis).
c) Connection to Leg 1 Lipids: Because Vitamins A, D, E, and K are non-polar (fat-loving), they require dietary lipids (fats) and bile salts in the small intestine to cross the intestinal villi into the body.
Keywords: water-soluble • fat-soluble • renal excretion • liver & adipose storage • hypervitaminosis
WORKBOOK QUESTION 2 GUIDE B-Complex vs. Vitamin C Jobs

How do B-Complex vitamins and Vitamin C perform different jobs inside an active human body?

a) B-Complex Vitamins = Energy Coenzymes: Vitamins B1 (Thiamin), B2 (Riboflavin), B3 (Niacin), B6, and B12 act as metabolic coenzymes inside the mitochondria. They help enzymes break down the glucose, fatty acids, and amino acids from Leg 1 into ATP cellular energy (plus B12 and Folate help build red blood cells).
b) Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid) = Collagen & Immune Shield: 1) Required to cross-link collagen fibers that keep blood vessels, tendons, skin, and gums strong.
2) Acts as a powerful water-soluble antioxidant that protects white blood cells during heavy exercise and boosts plant iron absorption in the gut.
Keywords: coenzyme • cellular respiration (ATP) • ascorbic acid • collagen synthesis • antioxidant

Minerals, Nutrient Synergy & Plant Phytochemicals

Unlike vitamins, minerals are inorganic elements from the periodic table (like Calcium, Iron, Magnesium, and Zinc). They are never destroyed by heat or cooking, and they work best when paired with synergistic partner nutrients!

Major Minerals (Need > 100 mg/day) Workbook Q3

Your body requires more than 100 milligrams per day of these minerals to build bone structure and run nerve/muscle electrical signals:

  • 🦴 Calcium (Ca2+) — RDA 1,000–1,300 mg/day: Hardens bone and tooth matrix (hydroxyapatite) and triggers actin-myosin muscle fiber contraction and blood clotting.
  • ⚡ Magnesium (Mg2+), Sodium (Na+) & Potassium (K+): Stabilize ATP energy molecules, relax muscles after contraction, and fire nerve action potentials.
Trace Minerals (Need < 100 mg/day) Workbook Q3

Your body requires less than 100 milligrams per day, but even a tiny shortage causes severe system failure:

  • 🩸 Iron (Fe) — RDA 11–18 mg/day: Sits at the core of hemoglobin in red blood cells (erythrocytes) and myoglobin in muscles to bind and carry oxygen (O2). Without iron, cells starve for O2 and cannot make ATP!
  • 🛡️ Zinc (Zn) — RDA 8–11 mg/day: Essential cofactor for over 100 enzymes, DNA repair, wound healing, and immune T-cell production.

OPTION A: THE "BIO-SHIELD" Antioxidant & Immune Focus

Cellular Antioxidant & Immune Recovery Matrix

Best For Client Personas: Cross-country endurance athletes, high-stress university students, healthcare shift workers, or post-illness recovery clients.

1. Key Micronutrients: Vitamin C (90–200 mg), Vitamin E (15 mg alpha-tocopherol), Zinc (10 mg), and Selenium (55 mcg).
2. Prairie Whole-Food Phytochemicals: Freeze-dried Saskatchewan Saskatoon Berry powder or wild blueberry extract (rich in purple anthocyanins), tart cherry powder, or ground flax hull lignans.
3. Body-System Mechanism (For Q9b): Donates electrons to neutralize exercise-induced free radicals, stops lipid peroxidation of muscle cell membranes, and fuels white blood cell immune defense.
OPTION B: THE "BONE & OXYGEN ENGINE" Structural & Aerobic Focus

Hemoglobin O2 Transport & Skeletal Density Matrix

Best For Client Personas: Teenage hockey/basketball players in winter, female distance runners, strength athletes, or Saskatchewan farm/construction workers.

1. Key Micronutrients: Iron (12–15 mg), Vitamin C (75 mg to triple plant iron absorption), Calcium (400–600 mg), Vitamin D3 (600–1,000 IU), and Magnesium (150 mg).
2. Whole-Food & Bioavailable Ingredients: Roasted pumpkin seed protein or red lentil flour (iron + magnesium), fortified oat/pea milk solids (calcium + Vit D3), and acerola/camu berry (Vitamin C).
3. Body-System Mechanism (For Q9b): Builds hemoglobin in red blood cells to carry O2 to mitochondria, while Vitamin D3 unlocks intestinal Ca2+ absorption to harden bone matrix and prevent stress fractures.
